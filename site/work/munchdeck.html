Munchdeck — Simplifying Food Ordering on Mobile

A focused redesign/prototype exploring a cleaner home-to-checkout flow, clearer order tracking, and a lightweight wallet experience for everyday food orders.

Overview

Munchdeck is a mobile-first food delivery prototype focused on reducing friction from discovery to post-order. The prototype emphasizes clarity, scannability, and realistic states (tabs, sheets, modals, toasts) while keeping the interaction model lightweight and predictable.

Problem Statement

Common pain points in food delivery apps include busy category discovery, unclear pricing/discount breakdowns at checkout, and order tracking that blends active and completed orders without clear filtering. Users need a way to move quickly through ordering while still having confidence in totals, payment method (wallet), and delivery status.

  • Discovery friction: Category browsing often competes with restaurant lists, making it hard to scan options quickly.
  • Checkout ambiguity: Totals, discounts, and delivery fees can be buried or unclear before placing an order.
  • Order management: Active, scheduled, and past orders are mixed together, making it difficult to find what’s in-progress vs. completed.
  • Post-order actions: Rating, and support are often disconnected from the order detail flow.

Project Goals

  • Simplify Home → Restaurant → Cart → Checkout into a linear, predictable path.
  • Make wallet actions (balance, fund, withdraw) obvious and safe with confirmation states.
  • Improve order clarity with All / Active / Past tabs and status-aware cards.
  • Close the post-order loop (Rate → feedback, Re-order, Share, Help) without breaking flow.
  • Build a realistic, clickable prototype that’s easy to review in-gallery and hand off as a stacked Figma import.

Design & Prototyping Process

Information Architecture & Flows

The core user flow focuses on a minimal path: discover restaurants (with category chips), view restaurant/menu, add items with quantity control, review cart (breakdown + totals), place order, track in My Orders, and complete with receipt actions (rating, re-order). Secondary flows (Wallet, Profile, Settings, Legal, Support) are kept modular and accessible from bottom nav/profile.

Interaction Design

  • Tabs: My Orders uses All/Active/Past filtering to separate in-progress from history; scheduled orders remain visible in All for context.
  • Sheets & Modals: Withdraw (withdraw), confirmation sheets, and permission modals keep destructive/secondary actions out of the main page flow.
  • Feedback: Lightweight toasts confirm actions (fund/withdraw, sent, rating submitted, share, sign-out) without blocking progression.
  • Micro-interactions: Subtle slide-up (sheetUp), pop-in (modalIn), toast animation keep the UI feeling responsive.

Visual Direction

Munchdeck uses a warm, food-forward palette anchored by Jollof (#E8452A) with supporting Gold (#F59E0B), soft neutrals, and ample white space. Typography pairs Nunito for display/headings with Inter for body to maintain legibility at mobile scale. Components favor rounded cards, pill chips, and clear primary CTAs.

Prototype Implementation

Built as a single-file interactive prototype with semantic screen IDs, shared helpers and a consistent 390×844 phone shell. This approach keeps the prototype portable, reviewable in-browser, and easy to stack into frames for design handoff.

Solution

The prototype organizes different interactive screens into seven focused galleries (Onboarding, Home, Restaurant, Order, Interactions, Profile & Help, Auth Flow), with the source of truth in the prototype.

  • Wallet: Wallet screen with balance and quick actions; Fund Wallet and Withdraw sheets with presets (₦1k/₦5k/₦10k/Max), bank selection, and a confirmation step that updates the wallet balance with toast feedback.
  • Orders: My Orders with All/Active/Past tabs, dynamic counts, status chips (OUT FOR DELIVERY, DELIVERED, SCHEDULED), scheduled items visible only in All as intended, and cards linking to Track Order and Order Detail.
  • Post-order: Order Receipt wired with Rate → Rate Order → Restaurant, Share (toast), and Help → Support. Rate Order supports star selection, optional feedback, and submit → returns to receipt with confirmation toast.
  • Profile & Legal: Settings groups Account (Name & email → Edit Profile, Change PIN, Address book → Saved Addresses), Notifications, and Sign out. Support links to Live Chat and a Privacy/Terms screen that swaps its body content.

Key Features

Category-first discovery

6 chips for quick filtering with persistent restaurant list context.

Transparent cart

Item breakdown, delivery, discount, and live total before placing order.

Wallet with safety

Fund/Withdraw flows with presets, bank selection, and confirmation sheet to reduce errors.

Order clarity

All/Active/Past tabs with correct visibility rules for scheduled orders and accurate counts.

Post-order loop

Receipt → Rate with star rating + feedback, Re-order, Share and Help, all fully wired.

Review-ready structure

Phone-shell galleries per flow, a standalone splash, and a stacked-frame export for handoff.

Core User Flows

  1. Discovery → Restaurant: Home → Restaurants list → Tap Mama’s Kitchen → Restaurant detail with menu items and quantity controls.
  2. Cart → Checkout: Add to Cart → Cart shows item list, breakdown (subtotal/delivery/discount)→ Place Order → Order Placed → Track Order / My Orders.
  3. Orders → Tracking/Receipt: My Orders → OUT FOR DELIVERY card → Track Order; DELIVERED card. Tabs filter correctly (All includes Scheduled; Active shows in-progress; Past shows delivered/cancelled).
  4. Post-order completion: Order Receipt → Rate (★) opens Rate Order → Select stars + optional feedback → Submit returns to the receipt with toast confirmation. Re-order returns to the restaurant; Share shows a toast; Help navigates to Support.
  5. Wallet management: Profile → Wallet → Fund Wallet or Withdraw. Withdraw uses GTBank ••••0432, amount presets, and a confirm sheet that updates the wallet balance and shows a success toast.
  6. Settings & Support: Settings → Name & email, Change PIN, Address book → Saved Addresses. Support → Live Chat, Privacy Policy / Terms of Service.

Outcome & Learnings

The prototype keeps the ordering flow tight while covering realistic edge states (tabs, sheets, ratings, wallet actions). Separating flows into galleries makes design reviews faster, and the single-file HTML + vanilla JS keeps it lightweight and easy to iterate. Key learnings: keeping one primary action per screen, surfacing breakdowns before commitment, and using tabs + explicit states (All/Active/Past) significantly improve scannability for order history.

“Clarity first: show the next action, make totals obvious, and keep post-order actions one tap away without cluttering the receipt.”
